\section{Purpose and decision status}

This document proposes the scope for a \textbf{Technical Reviewer
Observability \& Traceability} workspace for ClaimGuard AI: what we would
build, what we would explicitly defer, and what we would never build.

It is a \emph{filtering and decision} document over the proposed MoSCoW lists.
It is \textbf{not} a final architecture, \textbf{not} an implementation
specification, and \textbf{not} a commitment that telemetry already works ---
it does not. Telemetry export is currently inert (see Section~5).

Decision status: \textbf{PROPOSED}. Before any implementation starts, an ADR
must record the scope, ownership, and the open decisions in Section~11.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Technical Reviewer goal}

The goal is a \textbf{single product workspace} inside ClaimGuard from which a
Technical Reviewer can:

\begin{itemize}
\item see whether the service, its data sources, and its jobs are healthy and
      \emph{fresh} (not just ``up''),
\item investigate a claim flow end to end (which checks ran, what the engine
      produced, what the audit ledger recorded),
\item find and act on operational alerts, and
\item inspect the business traceability trail without touching raw systems.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Backend-only adapters.} The platform talks to
Prometheus, OpenTelemetry (OTLP), Grafana, Loki, Tempo, and Alertmanager
\emph{as applicable}, entirely from the backend. The browser never talks to
those systems directly. Every observability capability is served to the
frontend through ClaimGuard's own API, under the same privacy and audit
posture as the rest of the product.

\section{System observability versus business traceability}

These two concerns are deliberately separated. \textbf{Audit is not
telemetry}, and conflating them is a design error we are avoiding:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{System observability (telemetry):} metrics, logs, traces, and
      alerts about how the \emph{system} behaves. Volatile, high-volume,
      operational, and by default \emph{not} part of the business record.
\item \textbf{Business traceability (audit):} the immutable, hash-chained
      record of \emph{business and governance events} (who did what to which
      claim, when, and why). Append-only, integrity-protected, and the
      authoritative evidence trail.
\end{itemize}

One UI presents both, but each has its own source, permissions, and retention
semantics (Table~\ref{tab:audit-vs-telemetry}).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{} l L L @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Aspect} & \textbf{System observability} & \textbf{Business traceability} \\
\midrule
Definition & Metrics, logs, traces, alerts & Immutable claim / governance events \\
Source & OTel collectors, Prometheus, Loki, Tempo & \code{claimguard.audit\_events} (Postgres) \\
Integrity & Best effort, no hash chain & SHA-256 hash chain, append-only \\
Retention & Short window, configurable, no PII & Long-lived, per policy \\
Permissions & Ops-oriented role & Stricter, role-locked \\
Failure behaviour & Safe degradation (read-only) & Fails open on verification \\

\section{Principles}

These principles gate every decision in Sections~6 and~7.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Review, not adjudicate.} The workspace shows status, health,
      and evidence. It never approves, denies, or advises on claims.
\item \textbf{Privacy / synthetic-only.} All data in the workspace, its
      fixtures, and its demos is synthetic. No real patient or claim data.
\item \textbf{No PII/PHI in telemetry.} Logs, metrics, labels, traces, and
      alerts must never carry patient identifiers or clinical content.
\item \textbf{Low-cardinality metrics.} No per-claim or per-patient metric
      labels; aggregate at safe levels only.
\item \textbf{Server-side auth.} All observability and traceability routes are
      mounted behind the platform's (future) auth layer. The browser is never
      an authorized client of the telemetry systems.
\item \textbf{Safe degradation.} If a telemetry source is down, the workspace
      shows ``unknown / stale'' clearly; it must never fabricate status or
      block the review flow. Audit verification failure fails open.
\item \textbf{Tenant-ready correlation without deciding tenant enforcement
      now.} Correlation metadata (tenant-aware IDs, correlation IDs) is
      designed so a future \code{tenant\_id} can be attached and enforced
      later. No tenant model and no tenant filtering are implemented in this
      scope.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Impact / non-overlap notes:}

\begin{itemize}

      flow. B1/B2/B3 streams are involved only when their services need
      instrumentation (LLM provider health, benchmark runs, assistant metrics).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{UX focus:} the overview leads with \emph{top-level status} and

stale data is never mistaken for healthy data. Drill-down is the pattern;
dashboard clutter is not.

\section{Backend / frontend data flow and bounded API recommendations}

\textbf{Data flow:}
\begin{center}
\small
\code{frontend} $\to$ \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/*} (platform API) $\to$
backend adapters $\to$ Postgres / OTel collector (Prometheus, Loki, Tempo,
Alertmanager as applicable)
\end{center}
The frontend calls \emph{only} platform APIs --- never Grafana, Prometheus,
Loki, Tempo, or Alertmanager directly. Grafana remains a backend-consumed
store, not a browser-facing surface.

\textbf{Bounded API names (names only, no implementation):}
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/overview} --- aggregate status, active
      incidents, source freshness
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/health} --- component readiness
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/metrics} --- bounded time-series query
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/logs} --- redacted, searchable logs
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/traces} --- trace search and span view
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/transactions} --- claim-run / transaction
      index linking audit and telemetry
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/alerts} --- current alerts and incidents
\item \code{/v1/technical-reviewer/audit} --- audit trail search and chain
      verification
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contract constraints on every endpoint:} explicit pagination, bounded
time windows, query limits, and server-side redaction before anything reaches
the frontend. No endpoint returns raw payloads.

\section{Acceptance criteria and open decisions}

\textbf{Acceptance criteria (when the scope is approved and built):}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{AC1 --- Honest status.} The overview renders real source
      freshness; a stopped or silent source is shown as ``stale / unknown'',
      never as healthy.
\item \textbf{AC2 --- Redaction.} Logs and traces served to the workspace pass
      the sensitive-field allowlist; nothing unredacted leaves the backend.
\item \textbf{AC3 --- Integrity.} Traceability views show only hash-chained
      events; a verification failure is surfaced as an error (fails open).
\item \textbf{AC4 --- Audit coverage.} Every incident action
      (ack / assign / comment / resolve) produces an audit event.
\item \textbf{AC5 --- No direct access.} Browser code contains no references
      to Grafana / Prometheus / Loki / Tempo / Alertmanager endpoints.
\item \textbf{AC6 --- No PII/PHI.} No metric label, log field, trace attribute,
      or alert carries PII/PHI; enforced in review.
\item \textbf{AC7 --- Auth-ready.} Routes are defined to be mounted behind
      auth; the permission matrix is applied server-side once auth exists.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Open decisions (recorded in the Phase~0 ADR):}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{D1 --- Role permission matrix.} Who may see audit versus
      telemetry versus incidents.
\item \textbf{D2 --- Current vs.\ future tenant scope.} Tenant-ready metadata
      now; enforcement only with the tenant model.
\item \textbf{D3 --- Telemetry retention / location.} LGTM local stack vs.\
      managed; retention window per signal.
\item \textbf{D4 --- Correlation ID naming.} Reuse \code{audit\_events.trace\_id}
      convention; exact attribute name and format.
\item \textbf{D5 --- Alert source / ack semantics.} Which components may alert;
      ack ownership; re-alert behaviour.
\item \textbf{D6 --- Sensitive-field allowlist.} Which canonical fields are
      never logged or traced; redaction rules.
\item \textbf{D7 --- SLO targets.} API latency / error budget; freshness
      thresholds for ``stale``.
\item \textbf{D8 --- Grafana deep links.} Are bounded, backend-mediated deep
      links ever permitted? Default is no.
\item \textbf{D9 --- Legal / privacy review.} Retention, storage location, and
      cross-region implications of logs and traces.
\end{enumerate}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Recommendation}

\textbf{Proceed with an additive, read-only Technical Reviewer workspace
first}, starting at Phase~0 (ADR and contracts) then Phase~1 (overview +
health + source status). Explicitly \textbf{not in this scope}: the tenant
model and tenant enforcement, direct Grafana embedding (or Grafana as the
primary experience), self-healing, and broad payload access. Telemetry export
starts only in Phase~2, coordinated with SeniorDev, and no document in this
series should claim it works before then.
